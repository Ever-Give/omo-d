CHAPTER 9: FOUNDATION STONE

###

“So are you just going to stay looking like a copy of me, or can you transform back to what you normally look like?” I asked, trying and failing to look like I wasn’t feeling some tension about the fact that it was able to accurately copy my appearance.

“Oh, that! Sorry, it’s my first time meeting a third generation founder! I guess I got all carried away and forgot to undo the enchantments… One moment while I…”

The thing was messing with a square stone device it pulled from a pocket in its battleskirt. Out of sheer curiosity, I also checked my battleskirt but there was nothing in my pocket. Of course there was nothing in my pocket. I guess I just never considered anything interesting enough to want to take it with me. As the thing finally managed to pull a polished red stone from the center of the dark gray stone square, I felt a brief pressure weigh down on me and then suddenly release, as if something had been forced into a cage and then suddenly released before the door could trap it inside. 

This seemed to get the thing’s attention, as it paused whatever it was doing with its stone device and was watching me as I looked around. I imagine I must’ve had a funny look on my face or something as I felt the pull continue to build and then surrender over and over again, never quite revealing the direction of the flow. At least I’m able to sense it more clearly now. Perhaps I’ve wandered closer to the yarnball through all this exploring? The thing chuckled at me for a moment, but when it saw my expression, it quickly stopped and went back to whatever it was doing with that stone device. Every time it moved one of those gems around, I swear I could feel the flow a little bit more cleanly.

Once again the thing chuckled to itself, this time not bothering to stop when I shot it a look. “Do your ears help you sense magic power?”

“What? I uh… Well I’m not really sure how I do it, but I don’t think it’s something my body does. It’s like when I met Stella. I didn’t even know my own name, and I knew I’d never seen her before, but some part of me knew her as someone deeper than friend or family. I don’t really know how to explain it, but when we first met, there was a moment where I felt as if I was looking through her eyes and she was looking through mine. It’s not like I actually saw myself out of her eyes, but I just knew exactly how she was looking at me, and I felt that flowing both ways through the both of us… I can’t explain these things, they just happen.”

This seemed to get the thing’s attention, as it paused what it was doing entirely and just took a moment to really look at me. There was such energy and curiosity in its eyes, it was like it was dreaming of creating something bigger than anyone in the entire world. “So you were capable of commonality from the moment you awoke. Either your baseline is higher than expected, or…” I waited for the copycat to finish that sentence, but it never did.

“Or?” I asked, putting emphasis on how I was pushing for an answer.

“Unfortunately, telling you could create far more problems than it’s worth. Believe me, I want to tell you, but it’s just not something I can decide for myself.” The thing continued to arrange the gems on its stone device. I could tell how much effort it was putting into keeping a straight face instead of looking at me with something remourseful. It may be good at controlling its facial expressions, but it’s letting a lot of its inner world seep out. 

I wonder if it knows I can sense that kind of stuff? Having placed the gems in a repeating order of color around the far edges of the stone square, the copycat then put the red polished stone back in the center of the square stone device. Before me, I saw a burst of colors. More colors than I’d ever seen before. Some I didn’t even know were possible. It was all coming from a glowing ball no more than an inch across, but it was packed with magic power so dense I couldn’t describe it. In all honesty, it was like the sheer depth of it was trying to smother me. It’s not like it was violent or fierce… More like I felt like an ant looking at someone the size of myself.

“There are reasons we’re not allowed to reveal the process we use or the reasons behind our rules, but I can tell you this: if you touch this magic orb, you’ll come to know a power like you’ve never felt before.”

“I refuse.” This seemed to really catch the copycat off guard. “Earlier, you apologized for taking on the copy of me, then you told me you were going to undo the enchantments. So why do you still look like me?”

“Well I… Wait…” The thing took a moment to look at its hands. I watched it turn its hands over several times as if it was staring at someone else’s hands and had no recollection of what they looked like. Perhaps that was for the best, they’re just copies of my hands after all. “Why do I still look like you do? It should’ve undone by n-”

A tremor shook the room, causing us to both pause what we were arguing about and watch the dust fall from the ceiling and land right in front of us.

“Come with me!” The thing grabbed my hand and led me away. I pulled back expecting to easily be able to pull my hand free, but it was suddenly much stronger than it had been earlier.

As it ran through the halls, I saw brief flickers of light course along its skin. Finally, one of these flickers seemed to take root, causing it to suddenly let go of me and step back. I watched as the light became all-encompassing as it swallowed the copycat whole before it started to fade. I lowered my hand from my eyes and saw a creature that looked like what would happen if you took a catsii and replaced all the cat features with dog features. I… I felt strange… With humans, they feel far away and “distant”, but with this one… I couldn’t feel them at all. They were standing right in front of me, yet I couldn’t feel their soul.

“You look surprised.” It said as it looked at me with those mismatched eyes. One was amber on the inside and deep purple on the outside, while the other eye was the other way around. It had brown hair and its floppy houndlike ears were the same color, although its tail was slightly lighter than the hair on its head. It was tall, very tall. It had to have at least a foot on me, and I’ve learned through my travels that I’m not necessarily short. It had light peach skin and its arms were covered in scars. Despite the difference in skin tone, the scars looked to be exact copies of the scar on my cheek. How could two of us be so different, yet have exactly the same scar? Same color and everything, despite us having different colored skin.

“It’s… The same…” I said, not realizing it had fallen out of my mouth until I saw it react to my words.

“Yeah I saw you looking at my scars. Since I first met you, I’ve noticed that you’re very observant. I wouldn’t be surprised if you’re able to sense the activation of magic.”

“Can you tell me where you got your scars?” I asked, trying not to be too rude about the question, but I guess there’s no way to ask without it being awkward.

“From when I was reborn, just as you have been.”

“You mean I’m a reincarnation? If so, wouldn’t I remember growing up as a baby?”

“Not a reincarnation of a single person. The catfolk were a lot like the foxfolk are today. There were eight tribes of catfolk, and each was so different that you wouldn’t think they were even the same race if it weren’t for the feline features they all shared, and there was plenty of variety there too. Naturally, the tribes were too different for our plan to work the way we had envisioned it. So now we’re left picking up the pieces. Of the eight founders, it seems only three of you had a strong enough will to keep yourselves held together. Tell me, do you really think someone like Stella is able to put out that much raw power from a single soul?”

“Well I… To be honest, I haven’t seen Stella really use her power. She was hurt when we met, and her condition makes it so she can’t use much of her power at once.”

“Oh the magiglower thing. I don’t know who started that rumor, but it couldn’t be more wrong. She’s not at risk of burning up or anything dumb like that. It’s more like an identity crisis than an injury or illness.”

“So she’s not going to die before 40?”

“Of course not! Who told you that?”

“Oscar did.”

“Of course he did. I don’t know where he gets these ideas, but here’s the thing… Oscar is very intelligent, but he lacks the ability to sense the world around him. He tries to figure things out when he’s blind to most of the pictures he’s trying to describe.”

So then… An ember’s shadow… Was that also made up?

“Something else on your mind?”

“Well Oscar told me about how magic has equal and opposite forces, and that if it conflicts with the world around magic, there will be secondary reactions or shadows to balance it out.”

“Well… He’s not wrong, although that explanation leaves a lot up to interpretation. Long story short, he’s explaining something very real, but he’s missing a lot of how it actually works. It’s not something I’ll be able to explain in words, so why don’t I just show you?”

“You can do that? I haven’t seen a single reaction since I entered this place.”

“Because this place’s baseline magical index is so high. There’s so much magic power here, it’s like throwing a rock at a tidal wave and wondering why you didn’t see the ripple from the rock when the tidal wave crashed and knocked down a city. If you want to be able to see those kinds of reactions, you’ll need something powerful enough to disturb the flows that are already in motion.”

“And even Vermillian Nova wasn’t enough to do that?”

“Not even close. To be honest, even I have to really concentrate to be able to do it, and I just don’t have the power to spare right now.”

“And what are you saving that power for?”

“For you, of course. I’m a first generation founder. With the second generation a complete failure and only three of you third generation founders still holding together, it’s only natural I help where I can. After all, my life was never truly mine to begin with. Of all the first generation founders, I have the least power, yet I’m also the one who carries the key to the future.”

“You still haven’t told me what a founder even is. It’s getting annoying going round and round like this.”

“Okay so you know how commonality works? Like when you first met Stella and your souls brushed up against each other?”

“I mean, I know what I felt. I can’t say I can describe the mechanism behind it.”

“That’s fine. I’ll try to keep this simple. When a large group of similar people are all killed in the same way at the same time, their energies get all mixed together. The membranes that hold individual souls together break down over time, and it all pools together into one big whirlpool of energy. Within that whirlpool is the essence of every individual person in the group, but good luck actually finding any individuals once they get all mixed up like that. Look, there’s only so much I can tell you, but I can tell you that me stopping the explanation here is for your own good. If you can put two and two together on your own, then that’s your problem. I’m not going to be around long enough to have to worry about it anyway.”

“You won’t? Are you sick or something? Is that why I can’t sense you?”

“Ah, so you can’t feel me at all despite having such an observant and flexible soul. I knew I was running out of time, but it looks like I really am running on fumes here” He gave a sort of subdued chuckle as he reached into one of his pockets and produced another device I’d never seen before. This one looked like two metal claws held together by some kind of metal string. It had a gem at its base, but other than that I really couldn’t tell what I was looking at.

“Oscar is able to share his power. Maybe he could help you. You seem to know a fair bit about Oscar, surely you know where he is. If we find him, maybe we could help you out.”

“It is the duty of a foundation stone to lay itself upon the ground and provide a path for others to walk. Some day you’ll know what this means. For now, just know that I’m happy with the choices I’ve made. There’s no bringing my race back, as I’m the last founder of my race, but with you, there’s three of you. Even if there’s only the tiniest chance that it could still work, I’m happy to have the opportunity to lay down my life and offer my abilities to the current generation of founders. After all, you three are the last founders that will ever be born into this world. There’s no way to bring my race back, but that’s not what’s important. You three have that chance, and I can give you the first piece of the key towards achieving that goal.”

“So you’re not… You’re not even going to try to keep on living?”

“I’m a curse of contradiction. I’ve stopped being alive a long time ago, yet I’m still here. Split as though I am, I’m still here. Fractured and broken, yet some part of one of the many within me is still alive. I was never able to become a rising founder, but at least I can go out with a bang as a foundation stone. Nothing you can say will change my mind. All you need to know is that I possess the ability to split and combine souls. In your hands, that power could mean something. You might even be able to understand that abyss that keeps staring back at you.”

“Can we just take a second here? You’re talking like you’re about to disappear! There’s no way you could just go poof and vanish from the world! You’re right in front of me, talking to me, having an intelligent conversation with me. There’s gotta be something that can be done to help you! Just tell me where I can find Oscar and I’ll make sure he helps you out!”

“I just knew you’d say something like that. The trunk jumpers always were proud of their ideals and their selflessness. It seems some things never change over the centuries. Just promise me you’ll forgive yourself for what I’m about to do.”

I watched him raise the metal clawed thing as the string began to unwind and fall to the floor. For a brief moment, I could feel his soul. Such bliss. What a profound sense of purpose and completion. When the light faded and I could see again, I was back in the labyrinth. This time, I could see. I could move. I knew what I had to do. I couldn’t let Stella break the barrier on the door. Even if it meant I got buried in the aftermath, I had to break the doors down so the barrier would shatter before Stella could try to break it with her own power. I figured I’d start by punching it as hard as I could over and over. It wouldn’t matter if my knuckles broke, it wouldn’t matter if they collapsed onto me. I just had to get this done if I wanted to help Stella and Oscar. 

After all, the plan will only work if the three of us are all on the same wavelength. I don’t see any way for Stella to truly understand us if she doesn’t even understand herself. An identity crisis, huh? I wonder if that means there’s a part of her stuck down somewhere deep and dark, trying to cry out and be heard, but buried in the depths of the abyss. I wonder, has Stella ever stared at her own abyss long enough for it to stare back, or has she always pushed her power away?

With all that in my mind, I landed the first punch against the large stone doors. As I expected, it hurt. It hurt a lot, but it didn’t bother me. I felt supercharged. I don’t know how to describe it. I had a goal to complete and nothing was going to stop me. Not the blood running down my hands, not the lack of a single scratch on the stone doors, not the fact that the barrier is much stronger than I am. Punch after punch, my hands dug themselves into the stone. The doors were moving a little bit now, and I had managed to feel the beginnings of a rhythm. I punch, the doors swing a fraction of an inch and bounce back, and I punch again. This time, I punched with my left hand knowing it was the weaker of my two arms, and the moment the doors moved, I swung with my right arm. I made contact right as the doors bounced back and I felt the charge penetrate deep into me.

Every inch of my body was telling me to put everything into this one punch. I stepped back and got my footing, keeping myself from ever being completely still so that the charge wouldn’t fade, and then I firmly drove my right fist into the stone door before me. The impact sent a wave of pain through my hand and arm, forcing me to yell in pain as I withdrew my hand and held it in my left hand. I probably just broke my hand, but my attention was on the fall of the large stone door. As the dust cleared, I finally got a good look at its total size. It had to be at least eighty feet tall and over ten feet wide. Nothing to do now but wait. It’s probably not the best idea to start exploring a dungeon with a broken hand.

Where’s the real one?

I knew that voice. It was that dog guy that… He said he gave me something… But he’s not around anymore… I can’t just give this back, but if this is what it does to me, then I just want to throw it out.

“You could at least stop being so mean. You said you were going to help me.” I tried to free myself from this constricting feeling by slashing at the air and pressing my magic outwards, but it did nothing. If anything, it made the feeling worse.

“Oh please. I never said I would help you achieve your own goals. But now that I’m inside you, my goals have become your goals.”

I felt a deep pain. I can’t really describe it as a physical pain or an emotional pain. It just hurt. It felt like I was being completely swallowed up. So far, I've seen several copycats. I’ve seen copycats of my friends, my family, and even myself. I don’t know how to explain it, but this one was not a copycat. This one was me. I remember being starstruck and terrified by the jet black eyes that boasted glowing blue irises. The vibrant red ears and tails with black tips. The pale skin and black nails. The way she looked at me with such profound dismay, but also… Respect? I had already begun to dive into the founder’s eyes. I didn’t think anything could interrupt an invocation like that once it had already begun, yet here she was, just standing there, casting a shadow towards herself instead of away from herself.

“I see you can see me now” It said in a rather pleased but also disappointed tone. I couldn’t read its intentions, but somehow I knew it didn’t mean me any harm.

“Yes. I can see you…” I didn’t know what to say. I mean, what would you do if you suddenly saw a reversed copy of yourself, but it was so perfect and deeply connected that you just knew it was the only real one?

“Why do you fear me?”

Despite being so calm and so direct, yet also so open with how they let their energy flow into me, I could feel a tense contradiction. One part of them wanted to help me. That much I knew for sure. However, another part of them seemed unhappy with that idea. I could tell rather easily that the will to protect me was winning by miles, but what would happen if that ever changed? Could I ever truly trust a power so fierce and dense that it can manifest itself in front of me like this?

“I… I… I don’t know…” What else could I have said? Was I supposed to tell this thing to its face that I hate what it did?

I don’t know how I escaped the labyrinth and came back here, but as long as I’m here, I should finish what I started the moment I stepped into this maze. Time to break this curse and rip the yarnball apart. I looked at him, frozen in time in that split state of crying and smiling at the same time, and I thought about trying to use force since he couldn’t fight back so long as he wasn’t experiencing time, then I thought about what happened to me in the moment that time froze for me. Perhaps something similar was happening to him…

Somehow it was even more unsettling when that cursed split appearance of his faded and he returned to his normal appearance from earlier instead of him becoming violent. I’ve almost reached for that power before and I felt as if it would consume me, so I pulled back and hid it in a deep dark place I’d never be able to easily reach. To see it made real right in front of me like that, I didn’t know what to do. I guess I really was terrified.”

When I entered this place, I had a hard time telling what was going on, but as soon as I started communicating what I wanted, it found ways to give it to me, even if it was missing the most important pieces of what I actually needed. “I was looking for Stella, so it made another Stella. I must admit that I was surprised when he said that he was giving me his founder’s praise, not just because I know it meant a lot to him based on what I could feel, but also because of what I felt in the moment when time was paused. He came towards me with such determination in his eyes as he put a hand to my chin and forced my head up a bit higher, although I can’t say he did this with any intention of offending me. If anything, this was part of what was feeding that pleasant sense of completion that he’d been giving off. “Don’t be sad over something like me. Keep that chin up.
